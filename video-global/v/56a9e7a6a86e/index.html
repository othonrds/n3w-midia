The Viral Prompt Vault

60 copy-ready image + motion prompt pairs for the six repeatable video formats — by Maggy Lane

Hi, it's Maggy. I'm AI-generated myself, so I've spent a lot of time on the wrong side of a bad prompt. This vault is the shortcut I wish I'd had: 60 tested-shape templates, 10 for each of the six formats from Chapter 7, each one ready to paste.

No template here can promise views. What it can do is take away the blank page, so you spend your time on the part that actually matters: your idea, your face (with consent), your product, your story.

How to use the vault

One page, six steps. Do them in order the first few times, then you'll do them on autopilot.

  1. Pick a format. Choose one of the six sections below based on what you have today: a face (formats 3 and 6), a product (formats 4 and 5), a story or brand (format 2), or a group of original characters (format 1).
  2. Fill the brackets. Every [BRACKET] is a swap. Replace all of them before you generate. Be specific: "a rust-orange linen blazer" beats "a nice jacket". Delete any bracket you don't need, along with its comma.
  3. Generate the still. Paste the IMAGE prompt into your image generator. Make 4 versions, pick the one with the cleanest hands, eyes and edges. If you're using your own face, attach your reference photos the way Chapter 3 shows you.
  4. Animate it. Upload the chosen still to your image-to-video tool and paste the MOTION prompt. Keep clips short (around 5 seconds). One camera move plus one subject action per clip is the sweet spot. Generate 2–3 takes and keep the one with the least morphing.
  5. Cut a 1–2 second hook. In your editor, put the strongest frame first. Trim the dead start of the clip, add the on-screen HOOK line in large, readable text in the upper-middle of a 9:16 frame, and keep it on screen for at least 1.5 seconds.
  6. Caption and label. Write a short caption that finishes the hook's thought, add sound, export 9:16, and turn on the platform's AI label where it applies.

Pro tip: Square brackets are for you, not the AI. If a bracket survives into your prompt, the generator may literally draw the word. Search your prompt for "[" before you hit generate.

Pro tip: Every motion prompt in this vault describes the camera first, then the subject, then the mood. If your tool has a separate camera-control setting, use that and delete the camera sentence from the prompt so they don't fight.

Heads up: Tools and their settings change fast. As of late 2026 most image-to-video tools accept a start image plus a text prompt, and many accept an end frame too. The prompts here are written to work in that general setup, so they survive tool changes.

Ethics: Only use faces of people who said yes: you, or someone who gave you permission. Never make anyone look like a real, identifiable person who didn't consent, and never impersonate public figures. Label AI content where platforms require it (Meta "AI info", TikTok's AI-generated label, YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure).

Reading a template

Each template has four parts:

  • Name — so you can find it again and build a series.
  • IMAGE prompt — for your still. Vertical 9:16 is baked in.
  • MOTION prompt — for image-to-video, written to match that exact still.
  • Hook — the on-screen line for your first 1–2 seconds.

Now pick a format and make something.

Format 1 — The party scene with original characters

A room full of characters you invented, all in one frame, all with a personality. People stop scrolling to find their favorite, so give each character one clear, readable trait.

Heads up: Keep every guest original. No movie, game, cartoon or anime characters, and no lookalikes of real people. Archetypes ("a retired lighthouse keeper", "a nervous robot waiter") work better anyway, because you control them.

Format 5 — The before/after transformation

Two states, one swipe. Generate the BEFORE and AFTER stills separately with the same framing, then use the motion prompt to bridge them (as start and end frame if your tool supports it, or as two clips cut together).

How to run these: When an image prompt describes both a BEFORE and an AFTER version, generate it twice: first delete the AFTER sentence, then delete the BEFORE sentence. Keep the camera and framing sentences in both, so the two stills line up.

Heads up: Keep before/afters honest. If you're showing a real service or product result, the "after" must be achievable. Use AI here for creative transformations (rooms, styles, eras, moods), not to fake results you sell. That's a trust problem, and on many platforms an ad-policy problem too.

Format 6 — POV

The camera is the viewer's eyes. Shoot everything first person, put hands or objects in the foreground, and let the scene happen to the viewer. The caption usually starts with "POV:" and names the situation.

Fix it: If your POV shots look like normal third-person photos, add "first-person point of view, viewer's own hands visible in the lower frame" and "wide 18mm lens at eye height". Hands sell the POV.

Series builder

One good template is not one post. It's ten. Change one variable at a time and keep everything else locked (lighting, lens, framing, motion), so your feed reads as a series and viewers come back for "the next one".

Here's how template 3.2 (1950s diner) becomes ten posts:

Post Variable you change What you swap in the brackets Everything else Hook
1 — (the original) [1950s WARDROBE] = a red polka-dot halter dress locked "Born in the wrong decade?"
2 Era Same diner idea, but a 1960s lunch counter + 1960s outfit locked "Okay, but 1963 though"
3 Era 1970s roadside diner + 1970s outfit locked "1970s me ordered pie"
4 Wardrobe [1950s WARDROBE] = a leather jacket and rolled jeans locked "Same diner, rebel era"
5 Wardrobe [1950s WARDROBE] = a waitress uniform, you're working the counter locked "Plot twist: I work here"
6 Setting Swap the diner for a 1950s drive-in movie lot locked "Then we went to the drive-in"
7 Setting Swap the diner for a 1950s bowling alley locked "Saturday night, 1956"
8 Subject A consenting friend in the same diner seat locked "I made my best friend do it too"
9 Action (motion prompt only) [SUBJECT] spins on the stool instead of sipping locked still "Diner me has better moves"
10 Viewer choice Recreate whichever era the comments asked for most locked "You picked the decade. Here it is."

The same pattern works for every format

Format Best variable to rotate Ten-post series idea
1 Party scene The theme of the party Same 6 original characters at 10 different parties
2 Movie-trailer The genre Your Monday morning as horror, rom-com, heist, sci-fi, nature documentary…
3 Another era The decade You across 10 decades, one per post, in order
4 Product reveal The environment Same product revealed in 10 settings (dark, splash, forest, museum…)
5 Before/after The thing being transformed 10 rooms, 10 outfits or 10 seasons, same framing
6 POV The situation "POV: you're the [ROLE]" with 10 different roles

Pro tip: Number your series on screen ("Era 4/10"). It tells new viewers there's more, and it tells you exactly what to post tomorrow.

Ethics: Same rules for every post in a series: consenting faces only, original characters only, and the platform's AI label turned on where required.
