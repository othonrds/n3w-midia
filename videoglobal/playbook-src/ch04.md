# Chapter 4 — Pro portraits from selfies

*Your phone, a window and the right prompts: headshots, editorial portraits and restored family photos that look like a real photographer was there.*

## What you'll make

- A **corporate headshot** that still looks like you on a video call.
- A **creative editorial portrait** and a **lifestyle portrait** from the same selfie set.
- A friendly, honest **dating or social profile photo**.
- A **restored and colorized family photo**, done respectfully, with the original kept safe.
- Correctly sized exports for **LinkedIn, Instagram and your website**.

## The golden rule: garbage selfie in, plastic portrait out

AI can change your background, your outfit and your light. What it can't do well is invent the real you from a blurry, filtered, badly lit selfie. When the input is weak, the model fills the gaps with its idea of an average attractive face. That's where the "plastic" look comes from.

I'm AI-generated myself. I know exactly which details make a face read as real: pores, slight asymmetry, flyaway hairs, catchlights in the eyes. Those details have to exist in your input, or the model invents them, badly.

So we start with the camera, not the prompt.

## Step 1: Shoot input selfies that actually work

You need about 15 minutes, a window and your phone. No ring light required.

### The setup

1. **Face a window.** Daylight from the front or slightly to one side. Overcast days are perfect. Avoid direct midday sun.
2. **Turn off the overhead lights.** Mixed light sources give your skin two colors.
3. **Plain background.** A light wall works best. Clutter confuses background swaps.
4. **Use the rear camera if you can.** Prop the phone on books at eye level and use the self-timer or a voice shutter. Rear cameras are sharper and less likely to smooth your skin.
5. **Step back.** Hold or place the phone at least an arm's length away, then crop later. Close-up phone selfies stretch the nose and push the ears back; the AI will learn that distortion as your face. Many phones have a 2x lens: use it.
6. **Turn off beauty modes and filters.** Portrait mode is fine for viewing, but shoot the plain photo mode for inputs.

### The shot list (12–15 photos)

| Shot | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Front, neutral | Look into the lens, relaxed mouth | Master identity reference |
| Front, real smile | Think of something funny; teeth showing | Headshots need a natural smile |
| Front, closed smile | Lips together, slight smile | Corporate and editorial options |
| Three-quarter left and right | Turn head about 30 degrees, eyes to lens | Most flattering portrait angle |
| Profile left and right | Full side view | Helps tools understand your nose and jaw |
| Chest-up, shoulders angled | Body turned, face to camera | Classic headshot posture |
| Waist-up, natural pose | Hands visible, relaxed | Lifestyle shots and hand references |
| One different outfit | Repeat two shots | Teaches the tool what's you versus your shirt |

### Check before you prompt

Zoom in on each photo. Keep it only if:

- Your eyes are sharp.
- You can see skin texture (not smoothed to wax).
- Nothing covers your face: no hair across the eyes, no sunglasses, no hand.
- The color looks like you on a normal day.

Pick your best three: one front neutral, one front smile, one three-quarter. These are your **input set** for everything in this chapter. If you built a character card in Chapter 3, paste in your identity anchor too.

> **Pro tip:** Wear the outfit you'll want in the final image when you can. Swapping clothes works, but a real collar and real fabric around your real neck always looks more natural than a generated one.

> **Ethics:** Use your own selfies, or photos of someone who agreed to this specific use. Making a "professional photo" of a coworker, client or partner without asking is not a favor. Show them the result and delete it if they say no.

## Step 2: Choose your tool

As of late 2026, any chat-based image editor that accepts photo uploads can do this chapter. The free starting point I verified is the **Google Gemini app**: its image editing (the "Nano Banana" models) is designed to keep people looking like themselves across edits and to blend several uploaded photos into one scene, and it's free to try. Its images carry a visible watermark and an invisible SynthID watermark.

If you want a trained identity you reuse for months, Chapter 3 covers paid options such as Higgsfield Soul ID. For portraits, start free. Most people never need more.

The method below works in any tool that accepts reference images:

1. Upload your input set (front neutral first).
2. Paste the prompt.
3. Generate several options.
4. Pick the one where you'd recognize yourself instantly, not the prettiest one.

## Step 3: Corporate headshot

The goal is "competent, approachable, real." It should look like you would on a video call tomorrow, on a good day. If people meet you and feel surprised, the headshot failed.

```prompt
Use the attached photos as the identity reference. Keep my face exactly as it is: same facial structure, eye shape, nose, skin tone, skin texture, hairline and any marks or freckles. Do not slim, smooth or beautify. Keep my natural asymmetry.
Create a professional corporate headshot: chest-up, shoulders turned slightly to the [LEFT/RIGHT], face toward camera, warm confident closed-mouth smile.
Wardrobe: [OUTFIT, e.g. "navy blazer over a plain white crew-neck top"], well-fitted, natural fabric texture.
Background: smooth studio backdrop in [COLOR, e.g. "soft warm gray"], gently out of focus.
Lighting: large soft key light from front-left, subtle fill, soft catchlights in both eyes, no harsh shadows.
Camera: 85mm portrait lens, f/2.8, eye level, sharp focus on the eyes.
Photorealistic, natural skin with visible pores, true-to-life color, no text, no logos.
```

Variations to try:

- **Office background:** replace the backdrop with "a softly blurred modern office with plants and window light."
- **Outdoor:** "a blurred city street with green trees, late afternoon open shade."
- **Industry cues:** for creative fields, swap the blazer for a textured knit; for healthcare or trades, use your real work clothing.

> **Fix it:** Teeth look too white and too even? Add "natural tooth color and shape, keep my real smile" and use your smiling selfie as the first reference. Uniform glow-in-the-dark teeth are one of the fastest AI giveaways.

## Step 4: Creative and editorial portrait

This is the magazine-feature version of you. More mood, more personality, still unmistakably you.

```prompt
Use the attached photos as the identity reference. Same exact face, proportions, skin texture and hair. Do not beautify.
Create an editorial magazine portrait: [FRAMING, e.g. "waist-up, seated on a wooden stool, leaning slightly forward, forearms on knees"], looking just past the camera with a calm, thoughtful expression.
Wardrobe: [OUTFIT, e.g. "oversized charcoal wool coat over a black turtleneck"].
Set: [BACKGROUND, e.g. "deep teal painted wall with a subtle plaster texture"].
Lighting: single soft light from high camera-left, deep but gentle shadows on the right side of the face, moody low-key look.
Camera: 50mm lens, f/2, slightly below eye level.
Color: muted, rich tones, light film grain like a medium-format film photo.
Photorealistic, natural skin with pores and fine lines, no retouching, no text.
```

Moods to swap in, with what to change:

| Mood | Light | Color | Set idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moody | Single soft light, deep shadows | Muted, cool | Painted plaster wall |
| Bright and modern | Big soft frontal light | Clean, neutral | Seamless white |
| Golden-hour | Low warm sun from behind, rim light | Warm, glowing | Field or rooftop |
| Vintage film | Window light | Faded warm, heavy grain | Old armchair, patterned wallpaper |

## Step 5: Lifestyle portrait

Lifestyle portraits show you doing something. They work for "about me" pages, small-business websites and social posts, because they feel like a moment instead of a pose.

```prompt
Use the attached photos as the identity reference. Keep my face, body proportions, skin texture and hair exactly as they are. Do not beautify or slim.
Candid lifestyle photo: I am [ACTION, e.g. "arranging fresh flowers in a ceramic vase on a wooden counter"], looking down at my hands with a small relaxed smile.
Wardrobe: [OUTFIT, e.g. "rolled-up linen shirt in sage green, simple apron"].
Location: [PLACE, e.g. "a small bright flower shop with buckets of flowers in the background"].
Lighting: natural morning window light from the left, soft and warm.
Camera: 35mm lens, f/2.8, medium shot, slightly above eye level, documentary style, as if a friend took it.
Hands natural with five fingers each, relaxed and anatomically correct.
Photorealistic, natural color, no text, no logos, no brand names on any objects.
```

> **Pro tip:** Lifestyle shots look most real when you give hands a job: holding a mug, a tool, a notebook. Empty hands floating in frame are where AI tends to get weird.

## Step 6: Dating and social profile photos

The point of a dating or social photo is to look like yourself on a great day. Not like a different, smoother person. Many dating apps also ask for real, current photos of you, so check each app's rules on edited images before you upload anything.

My honest advice: use AI here for **light, background and framing**, not to change your face or body.

```prompt
Use the attached photo as the base. Keep my face, body, skin, hair and age exactly as they are. Do not change my features, weight, height or skin texture.
Improve only: lighting (soft, warm, flattering natural light), background (replace with [SETTING, e.g. "a relaxed outdoor café terrace with blurred greenery"]), and framing (chest-up, slightly off-center, rule of thirds).
Expression and pose stay as in the original.
Style: natural candid smartphone-quality photo taken by a friend, true-to-life color, no heavy filter, no text.
```

Good profile photo set:

1. A clear, smiling face shot (this one).
2. A full-length shot doing something you actually enjoy.
3. A shot with context: your hobby, your city, your dog.

> **Ethics:** On dating and social apps, an AI-improved photo is fine; an AI-invented you is not. If someone would feel misled when they meet you, dial it back. Many platforms ask you to disclose realistic AI-generated content; when in doubt, label it.

## Step 7: Background and wardrobe swaps

Sometimes you love the photo and just hate the background, or the shirt. Change one thing at a time.

### Background only

```prompt
Keep the person in the attached photo completely unchanged: face, hair, body, clothing, pose, expression and lighting on the person.
Replace only the background with [NEW BACKGROUND, e.g. "a softly blurred bookshelf in a warm home office"].
Match the new background's light direction and color temperature to the light already on the person ([LIGHT DIRECTION, e.g. "soft light from the left"]).
Add a natural soft shadow where needed. Realistic depth of field, background slightly out of focus. No text, no logos.
```

### Wardrobe only

```prompt
Keep the person in the attached photo completely unchanged: face, hair, skin, body shape, pose, expression, background and lighting.
Change only the clothing to [NEW OUTFIT, e.g. "a camel-colored wool blazer over a cream silk blouse"].
The new clothes fit naturally on this exact body, with realistic folds, seams and fabric texture, and the same lighting and shadows as the rest of the image.
Keep the neckline, hands and hair edges natural where they meet the fabric. Photorealistic, no logos.
```

> **Fix it:** The new background looks pasted on? The light doesn't match. Look at which side of your face is brighter and say it in the prompt. Then ask for "matching color temperature" and "a soft contact shadow." Mismatched light is the number one cutout tell.

## Step 8: Restore and colorize old family photos

This is the most emotional thing in this book. A torn photo of a grandparent, brought back so the whole family can see their face again. Do it carefully.

### First, protect the original

1. **Scan, don't snap, if you can.** A flatbed scanner at 600 dpi or more, saved as PNG or TIFF, gives the AI real detail to work from. No scanner? Photograph the print flat, in even daylight, with the phone held perfectly parallel and no glare.
2. **Keep the untouched scan forever.** Name it "ORIGINAL." Never overwrite it.
3. **Restore first, colorize second.** Two separate steps give you more control and fewer hallucinations.

### The tools (as of late 2026)

| Tool | What it does | Cost tier |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini app (Nano Banana) | Prompt-based repair, cleanup and colorizing | Free to try, paid tiers |
| MyHeritage photo tools | Photo Enhancer for blurry faces, plus colorizing (and a repair tool for scratches) | Limited free use, subscription for more |
| Adobe Photoshop Neural Filters | Photo Restoration (labeled beta by Adobe) and Colorize filters | Paid |

Chat-based editors are flexible but can invent details. Dedicated restoration tools are more conservative. For a precious photo, try both and compare.

### Gentle repair prompt

```prompt
This is a scanned old family photograph. Restore it conservatively, like a professional archival restorer.
Remove scratches, dust, creases, tears and stains. Reduce fading and improve contrast gently. Sharpen slightly.
Do NOT change anyone's face, expression, age, features, hairstyle or clothing. Do not add or remove people or objects. Do not invent details that are not visible; if an area is missing, fill it softly and plainly to match the surroundings.
Keep the original film grain and the photographic look of the era. Keep it [black and white / sepia, same as original].
Output the full photo with the original framing.
```

### Respectful colorize prompt

```prompt
Colorize this restored black-and-white family photograph from around [DECADE, e.g. "the 1950s"], taken in [PLACE, e.g. "a small coastal town"].
Use realistic, slightly muted colors typical of color film from that era, not modern saturated digital color.
Skin tones natural and varied, matching each person's apparent complexion; do not lighten or change anyone's skin.
Known colors: [ANY FAMILY DETAILS, e.g. "the woman's dress was dark green; the man had light brown hair"]. For anything unknown, choose plausible, understated colors.
Do not change faces, features, expressions, clothing shapes or the background. Keep the original grain and softness.
```

> **Ethics:** Restoration should recover a face, not replace it. Compare the result to the original side by side. If a nose, a smile or an eye shape changed, that's no longer your relative. Tell your family which parts are AI-colored guesses, keep the original with the restored version, and don't present a colorized image as a historical record. And remember: many people in old photos can no longer consent. Treat their faces with the same care you'd want for your own.

> **Heads up:** Animating old photos so ancestors "move" or "speak" is possible with some tools. Some families love it; others find it upsetting. Ask before you share it in the family chat.

## Step 9: Realism fixes for skin, eyes and hands

Even good outputs need a final check. Zoom to 100% and look at these three areas. Chapter 8 covers every AI tell; these are the portrait-specific ones.

### Skin

**Problem:** waxy, airbrushed, poreless.
**Fix:** ask for texture back, gently.

```prompt
Keep this exact image, same person, same face, same composition, same lighting and colors.
Only improve skin realism: restore natural skin texture with visible pores, fine lines around the eyes and mouth, slight natural redness and unevenness, and a few tiny imperfections. Remove the airbrushed, plastic look.
Do not change any facial features, shape or age. Add very subtle film grain over the whole image.
```

### Eyes

**Problems:** mismatched pupils, eyes looking in two directions, missing or doubled catchlights, irises with odd patterns.
**Fixes:**

- Ask for "both eyes looking at the same point, matching pupil size, one soft catchlight in each eye in the same position."
- Make sure your input selfie has sharp eyes. Blurry eyes in, strange eyes out.

### Hands

**Problems:** extra or merged fingers, rubbery knuckles, hands that don't grip objects.
**Fixes:**

- Give hands a simple task (holding a mug, resting on a table).
- Crop tighter so hands leave the frame if they don't add anything.
- Use a waist-up selfie with your real hands as a reference.

```prompt
Keep this exact image unchanged except for the hands.
Fix the [LEFT/RIGHT/BOTH] hand(s): five fingers each, natural proportions, realistic knuckles, nails and skin creases, relaxed natural grip on the [OBJECT].
Match skin tone, lighting and shadows to the rest of the person. Do not change the face, clothing or background.
```

### Final realism checklist per image

| Check | Looks real | Looks AI |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Pores, slight unevenness | Smooth wax, uniform glow |
| Eyes | Matching catchlights, same direction | Different reflections, wandering gaze |
| Teeth | Natural shade, slight variation | Bright white uniform strip |
| Hair | Flyaways, natural hairline | Helmet edge, painted strands |
| Hands | Five fingers, real grip | Extra fingers, floating |
| Light | One clear direction | Light from everywhere |

## Step 10: Export for each platform

Sizes change, so these reflect common recommendations as of late 2026. Always upload a bit larger rather than smaller, and check each platform's help page if something looks cropped.

| Where | Use | Size (pixels) | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Profile photo | 400 × 400 or larger | Square (shown as a circle) |
| LinkedIn | Banner | 1584 × 396 | 4:1 |
| LinkedIn | Portrait post | 1200 × 1500 | 4:5 |
| Instagram | Profile photo | at least 320 × 320 | Square (shown as a circle) |
| Instagram | Portrait feed post | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 |
| Instagram | Grid-safe portrait | 1080 × 1440 | 3:4 |
| Instagram | Stories and Reels | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |
| Website | About-page portrait | about 1200 × 1500 | 4:5 |
| Website | Hero or banner | about 2400 × 1200 | 2:1 |

Export tips:

1. **Crop for the circle.** Profile photos are shown round. Keep your face centered with space above the head, and nothing important in the corners.
2. **Save JPG at high quality** for photos. Use PNG only if you need transparency.
3. **Keep files light for websites.** Compress to a few hundred KB so pages load fast; most free image compressors do this in one click.
4. **Upscale only at the end.** If you need more pixels, upscale the final, chosen image once. Upscaling earlier exaggerates AI artifacts.
5. **Use the same headshot everywhere.** One recognizable photo across LinkedIn, your site and your email signature builds trust faster than five different looks.

> **Ethics:** If a portrait is AI-generated or heavily AI-edited and platforms require it, label it. As of 2026, Meta uses an "AI info" label and an AI disclosure control for photorealistic synthetic content, TikTok has an "AI-generated content" toggle, and YouTube asks for "altered or synthetic" disclosure in Studio. A headshot with a swapped background is usually a minor edit; a fully generated scene of you somewhere you've never been is the kind of thing worth disclosing. When in doubt, say so.

## Try it now

**15 minutes: from window selfie to LinkedIn-ready headshot.**

1. (5 min) Stand facing a window, rear camera at eye level on a stack of books, 2x lens if you have it. Shoot front neutral, front smile and three-quarter.
2. (5 min) Upload all three to a free tool that accepts photos, paste the corporate headshot prompt from Step 3, and fill in your outfit and background color. Generate four options.
3. (3 min) Pick the one your best friend would recognize instantly. Zoom in and run the realism table: skin, eyes, teeth, hands, light. Fix one thing if needed.
4. (2 min) Crop to a square with your face centered, export at 400 × 400 or larger, and put it side by side with your current profile photo.

If it looks like you on your best day, you're done. Next chapter, we do the same trick for products: a whole photoshoot from one phone photo.
