# Chapter 8 — Make it not look AI

*The six tells that give AI away, what causes each one, and the exact fix, from someone who is made of them.*

## What you'll make

- A "tell scan" habit: a 60-second check you run on every image and clip
- Repaired versions of your own flawed images (skin, hands, teeth, text, light)
- Video clips cut and re-generated so faces stop drifting and objects stop melting
- Captions and voiceover that sound like a person wrote and said them
- A posting routine that includes the right AI label on Meta, TikTok and YouTube

I'm AI-generated, so I'll be blunt: I know every one of these tells because I've had all of them. Waxy cheeks. Seven fingers. Light that came from nowhere. A face that slowly turned into my cousin halfway through a clip.

The good news is that the tells are predictable. Each has a cause, and each cause has a fix. "Not looking AI" doesn't mean hiding that you used AI (we'll get to labels at the end). It means the work looks *crafted* instead of *default*.

## The 60-second tell scan

Before you fix anything, learn to see it. For every output:

1. Look at it at full size, then at phone size. Some tells only show up small (light), some only big (skin, text).
2. Check, in order: skin → hands → teeth → any text → light direction → background edges.
3. For video, scrub frame by frame through the first and last second, and anywhere something passes in front of a face.
4. Flip the image horizontally. A fresh view makes asymmetry and odd anatomy jump out.

## Tell 1 — Plastic skin

**What it looks like:** Poreless, waxy, airbrushed skin. Uniform shine on forehead and nose. Faces that look like a phone-filter ad.

**What causes it:** Image models drift toward "average beautiful," and words like *beautiful, flawless, perfect, stunning* push them further. Heavy upscaling and beauty filters smooth what little texture was left. Too-soft, frontal light hides the micro-shadows that make pores visible.

**The fix:**
1. Delete flattery words from your prompt. Describe the *camera* and the *light*, not the beauty.
2. Ask for texture explicitly: pores, fine lines, peach fuzz, slight redness, uneven tone.
3. Use side or window light so texture casts tiny shadows.
4. Upscale with a low "creativity" or "detail invention" setting, or skip upscaling on faces.
5. Add light film grain in your editor. Grain gives the eye texture to read.

```prompt
Candid portrait of [SUBJECT], natural skin with visible pores, fine lines around the eyes, faint freckles, slight redness on the nose and cheeks, a few flyaway hairs, no makeup retouching. Soft window light from the left creating gentle shadows on the skin, slight overexposure on the window side. Shot on a 50mm lens at f/2.8, subtle film grain, true-to-life color, not airbrushed, not glossy.
```

```prompt
Regenerate only the selected face area. Keep the same person, same expression, same angle and same light. Replace the smooth plastic skin with realistic skin texture: visible pores, fine lines, natural uneven tone, a little shine only on the nose bridge. Do not change facial features.
```

> **Fix it:** If one image is perfect except the skin, don't regenerate the whole thing. Use your tool's region edit (often called inpaint, vary region or generative fill) on the face only, with the prompt above.

## Tell 2 — Hands, teeth and text

**What it looks like:** Extra or fused fingers, hands that melt into objects, a perfect row of identical "piano key" teeth, and signs or labels with gibberish letters.

**What causes it:** Hands and teeth are small, repetitive structures with huge variation across poses. Text is a precise pattern that image models approximate as "text-shaped." The more of these the frame contains, and the smaller they are, the more likely one fails.

**The fix:**
1. **Simplify the pose.** Ask for hands holding one clear object, relaxed at the side, or in pockets. Avoid interlaced fingers and hands near faces.
2. **Close the mouth or soften the smile.** "Closed-lip smile" or "slight smile, lips together" removes the teeth problem entirely.
3. **Never generate important text in the image.** Ask for a blank sign, label or screen, then add text in your editor.
4. **Crop it out.** A tighter frame that loses a bad hand is a legitimate fix.
5. **Regenerate the region** for hands and teeth when the rest of the image is a keeper.

```prompt
Portrait of [SUBJECT] with a relaxed closed-lip smile, one hand resting naturally on [a coffee cup] with all five fingers clearly visible and separated, the other hand out of frame. 50mm lens, waist-up framing, soft daylight, natural skin texture, realistic anatomy.
```

```prompt
Regenerate only the selected hand. A natural relaxed human hand with exactly five fingers, correct knuckles and fingernails, thumb on the near side, gently holding [OBJECT]. Match the skin tone, lighting direction and sharpness of the rest of the image.
```

```prompt
[SCENE DESCRIPTION] with a completely blank [shop sign / product label / phone screen], clean surface ready for text to be added later, no letters, no numbers, no symbols, no logos anywhere in the image.
```

## Tell 3 — Too-perfect light

**What it looks like:** Everyone is evenly lit from every side. No shadows under the chin. A golden glow with no visible source. A face lit from the left in a room where the window is on the right.

**What causes it:** Without lighting instructions, models default to flattering, shadowless "beauty light." They don't simulate physics; they average many images. So light direction, color and source often disagree with the scene.

**The fix:**
1. **Name one key light and its direction.** "Window light from the left," "single overhead bulb," "low sun behind the subject."
2. **Name a motivated source** the viewer can see or infer: a lamp, a window, a screen, a neon sign.
3. **Allow imperfection:** a blown-out window, a deep shadow side, mixed color temperatures.
4. **Match light across shots.** In a sequence, copy the same light sentence into every prompt.
5. **Grade it in the editor:** pull highlights down slightly and add a gentle vignette.

```prompt
[SUBJECT AND SETTING]. Lit only by [a single tungsten desk lamp] on the right side of the frame: warm light on the right half of the face, the left half falling into deep shadow, visible light falloff on the wall behind. Cool, dim blue ambient light from a window out of frame. Practical lamp visible in the shot. Realistic contrast, imperfect exposure, no fill light, no rim glow.
```

## Tell 4 — Face drift

**What it looks like:** Your character's face changes between images, or slowly morphs during a clip. Eyes widen, the jaw softens, they get younger or more "generic."

**What causes it:** Every new generation re-imagines the face from the prompt and reference. Small inconsistencies (angle, lighting, a vague description) add up. In video, the model invents each new frame from the previous ones, so errors compound over time, especially when the face turns, gets small in frame, or is briefly covered.

**The fix:**
1. **Always use a reference.** A clean, front-facing, evenly lit reference image (Chapter 3) beats any description.
2. **Repeat the identity anchors** in every prompt: age, face shape, eye color, hairline, one distinctive mark.
3. **Keep the face big.** Faces under about a fifth of the frame drift most.
4. **Shorter clips.** Generate 3–5 seconds and cut before the drift starts.
5. **Start every shot from a still you approved.** Never chain the last frame of a drifted clip into the next one.
6. **Avoid big head turns and hands crossing the face** unless the shot needs them.

```prompt
Same person as the reference image: [AGE]-year-old [DESCRIPTION], [FACE SHAPE] face, [EYE COLOR] eyes, [HAIR: color, length, parting], [ONE DISTINCTIVE DETAIL, e.g. small scar on left eyebrow]. Keep identity exactly the same as the reference. [NEW SCENE, POSE AND LIGHT]. Face clearly visible and large in frame, 85mm lens.
```

```prompt
Subtle motion only. The person blinks naturally and breathes, a slight smile forms, head stays facing the camera with less than 10 degrees of movement. No hands near the face. Camera static. Face, hairline and features must stay identical to the first frame for the entire clip. Duration 4 seconds.
```

## Tell 5 — Video morphing

**What it looks like:** Objects melt, a mug becomes a bowl, a door handle slides across the door, extra limbs appear in a crowd, backgrounds swim, people pass through each other.

**What causes it:** Video models are predicting plausible motion, not tracking real objects in 3D. Complex prompts (many moving things, fast camera plus fast subject motion), long durations, occlusion and fine repeating patterns all raise the odds of morphing.

**The fix:**
1. **One motion per shot.** Either the camera moves or the subject does. Rarely both.
2. **Describe slow, physical motion.** "Slow dolly-in," "turns her head slowly." Vague words like "dynamic" invite chaos.
3. **Tell the model what must not change:** "the mug shape stays rigid," "no new people appear."
4. **Simplify the background.** Fewer people and fewer patterns mean fewer things to melt.
5. **Cut earlier.** Most clips are cleanest at the start. Use the first 1.5–3 seconds and cut on action into the next shot.
6. **Hide seams with cutaways:** a detail shot, a cover cut, a beat cut (Chapter 7).

```prompt
Camera static. [SUBJECT] slowly lifts [OBJECT] from the table and brings it toward the camera. Only the hand and the object move. The object keeps exactly the same shape, size and color throughout, completely rigid, no warping. Background stays still, no new people or objects appear. Natural speed, no slow motion. Duration 3 seconds.
```

```prompt
Slow, smooth dolly-in toward [SUBJECT], who stays still except for natural breathing and one blink. All architecture and furniture remain straight and rigid, no bending lines, no flickering textures. Even, steady lighting with no flicker. Duration 4 seconds.
```

> **Pro tip:** Regenerate the same shot 3–4 times and pick the cleanest one. Then trim it. Cheaper than trying to rescue a melted clip in the edit.

## Tell 6 — Robotic captions and voice

**What it looks like and sounds like:** Captions in perfect, stiff sentences ("Unlock your potential with this amazing transformation!"). A voiceover with even pacing, no breaths, wrong stress on words, and a tone that never changes.

**What causes it:** Text generators default to polished marketing language. Text-to-speech defaults to neutral delivery and reads punctuation literally. Nobody talks the way a press release reads.

**The fix (captions):**
1. Write how you'd text a friend. Contractions, fragments, one idea per line.
2. Cut every "unlock," "elevate," "game-changer," "dive in."
3. Keep on-screen lines to 2–5 words.
4. Proofread auto captions yourself; they mishear names and invented words.

**The fix (voice):**
1. Write the script for the ear: short sentences, natural fillers in moderation ("okay, so"), pauses marked with line breaks or ellipses.
2. Generate 2–3 takes and splice the best lines.
3. Add room tone or very low music under the voice so it doesn't float in silence.
4. Better still: record yourself on your phone in a quiet room with soft furnishings. A real voice with a small stumble beats a perfect synthetic one.

```prompt
Rewrite this caption so it sounds like a real person talking to a friend, not an ad. Use contractions, short fragments, and plain words. No exclamation marks, no words like "unlock", "elevate", "game-changer", "stunning" or "transform". Maximum 3 short lines, each under 8 words. Keep the meaning:
[PASTE YOUR CAPTION]
```

```prompt
Rewrite this voiceover script for a 15-second vertical video so it sounds natural when spoken aloud. Short sentences. One or two natural pauses marked with "...". One casual opener like "okay, so" or "here's the thing". Emphasis on [KEY WORD]. No marketing clichés. Read time under 15 seconds:
[PASTE YOUR SCRIPT]
```

> **Ethics:** Only clone or synthesize a voice that is yours or that you have explicit permission to use. Never imitate a real person's voice.

## Troubleshooting table

| Problem | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Waxy, poreless skin | Flattery words, soft frontal light, heavy upscaling | Remove "beautiful/flawless", add texture words, side light, low-creativity upscale, add grain |
| Six or fused fingers | Complex pose, small hands in frame | Simplify pose, give hands one object, region-regenerate, or crop |
| "Piano key" teeth | Wide smile requested or implied | "Closed-lip smile"; region-regenerate mouth |
| Gibberish text on signs/labels | Models approximate text as shapes | Generate blank surfaces, add text in the editor |
| Light comes from nowhere | No light direction in prompt | Name one key light, its side and a visible source |
| Shadows don't match between shots | Each prompt describes light differently | Copy one identical light sentence into every prompt in the sequence |
| Character's face changes between images | No reference, vague identity | Use a reference image and repeat identity anchors every time |
| Face morphs mid-clip | Long clip, big head turn, small face, occlusion | 3–5 s clips, small movements, face large in frame, cut before drift |
| Objects melt or change shape | Too much motion, complex scene | One motion per shot, "rigid/no warping" instruction, simpler background |
| Background swims or breathes | Fine repeating patterns, camera plus subject motion | Plainer background, static camera or static subject |
| Extra people appear in crowds | Model invents plausible fillers | "No new people appear", fewer extras, shallower depth of field |
| Clip looks good, ending is weird | Errors compound over time | Use the first 60–70% of the clip; cut on action |
| Everything looks "too clean" | Default sharpness and saturation | Lower saturation slightly, add grain, a touch of vignette, slight imperfection in prompt |
| Product looks different from the real one | Model "improves" shapes and materials | Use a real product photo as reference; reject any output that changes it |
| Sharp face, mushy hair edges | Upscaler or background removal artifacts | Upscale before cutting out; feather edges; keep natural backgrounds |
| Captions misspell words | Auto captions mishear names and invented words | Proofread every line manually before export |
| Voice sounds flat and robotic | Neutral TTS defaults, written-style script | Rewrite for the ear, mark pauses, splice best takes, or record your own voice |
| Video looks soft after posting | Wrong export size or frame rate | Export 1080 × 1920, MP4 (H.264 + AAC), 30 fps; don't upscale low-res clips in post |
| Text hidden by app buttons | Placed outside the 9:16 safe zone | Keep text in the center band (Chapter 7 safe zones) |
| Colors jump between clips | Different generations, different grades | Apply one color grade or filter to the whole timeline |

## The 20-point pre-post check

Run this before every post. It takes about three minutes.

**Image and frame**
1. Skin has visible texture at full size.
2. Every visible hand has five sensible fingers.
3. Teeth look natural, or the mouth is closed.
4. No gibberish text anywhere, including in the background.
5. Light has one clear direction that matches the scene.
6. Shadows fall the same way across every shot.
7. The face matches my reference in every shot.
8. Product shape, color and label match the real product.
9. Background edges (hair, fingers, objects) are clean.
10. Nothing in frame resembles a real brand, logo, celebrity or franchise.

**Motion and edit**
11. No melting, morphing or shape-shifting objects.
12. No faces drifting; clips are trimmed before any drift starts.
13. No extra people or limbs appearing in the background.
14. Cuts happen on action or on the beat.
15. The hook is readable on frame one, on mute.

**Text, sound and export**
16. Captions are proofread and written like a person talks.
17. Voice sounds natural, or it's my real voice.
18. Music and sound effects are licensed or from the platform library, and levels are balanced.
19. All text sits inside the 9:16 safe zone; export is 1080 × 1920, MP4, 30 fps.
20. The right AI disclosure is switched on for the platform I'm posting to.

## Platform AI labels and honest disclosure

Making work that doesn't *look* fake is craft. Pretending it isn't AI is something else. I'm openly AI-made, and it hasn't hurt me once. Here's where things stand as of late 2026. Rules shift, so check each platform's help center before you post.

### Meta (Instagram, Facebook, Threads)
- Meta shows an **"AI info"** label (it replaced the older "Made with AI" tag in 2024).
- It can be applied automatically when your file carries industry-standard AI metadata such as **C2PA** Content Credentials or IPTC data, which many generators embed.
- If you post **photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created or altered, you're expected to disclose it using Meta's built-in AI disclosure option when posting. Meta may apply penalties when that's skipped.
- Ads have their own disclosure rules, and social issue, election and political ads have stricter ones.

### TikTok
- Creators can turn on the **"AI-generated content"** label from the post screen's more-options settings.
- Labeling is expected when content shows **realistic** people or scenes that viewers could mistake for real. Clearly stylized or cartoon content generally isn't the target.
- TikTok also reads **C2PA** Content Credentials and can label content automatically on upload.
- Ads use a separate AI-content disclosure in TikTok Ads Manager.
- Undisclosed realistic AI content can be labeled, removed or lead to account restrictions.

### YouTube (including Shorts)
- Creators must disclose **realistic altered or synthetic** content in YouTube Studio when uploading.
- On Shorts the label appears as an overlay on the video; on long-form, below the player.
- As of a May 2026 update, YouTube can also apply the label automatically when its systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, and some labels are permanent (for example, content made with YouTube's own AI tools or with C2PA metadata showing it's fully generated).
- YouTube says the label itself doesn't change recommendations or monetization.

### My simple rule
If a reasonable viewer could believe it was filmed or photographed for real, **label it**. If it's obviously stylized, labeling is still a nice courtesy. Never remove or strip metadata to dodge a label.

> **Ethics:** Disclosure is about trust, not shame. Label realistic AI content, use only faces and voices you have permission to use, and never make content that puts words in a real person's mouth.

## Try it now

**15 minutes: rescue one bad image.**

1. (2 min) Pick your worst AI portrait: plastic skin, odd hand, too-perfect light.
2. (3 min) Run the tell scan and write down every tell you find.
3. (6 min) Fix the biggest two: region-regenerate the skin or hand with the prompts above, or regenerate the whole image with the Tell 3 light prompt.
4. (4 min) Add light grain, place old and new side by side, and look at both at phone size.

The difference you see is exactly what your audience feels without knowing why.
