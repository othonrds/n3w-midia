# The AI Photo & Video Playbook — by Maggy Lane (writer's brief)

Product: a paid digital guide (web-book + PDF), English (US), sold globally (US/CA/UK/AU) for US$ 9.90–19.99.
Promise: "Create pro photos and cinematic videos with AI that don't look like AI — no camera, no studio, no editing experience."
Buyer: creators, freelancers, small business owners and curious beginners who already tried AI and got plastic, fake-looking results. They are not engineers. They want results this weekend.

## Voice
- Written by Maggy Lane, an openly AI-made creator ("I'm AI-generated myself, so I know every tell"). First person, warm, direct, a little witty, never hype.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences). Concrete verbs. No filler, no "in today's digital landscape".
- Teach by doing: every section ends with something the reader can make right now.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)
1. NO income or results promises ("make $X", "go viral guaranteed"). Say what they will be able to make, not what they will earn.
2. NO real people, celebrities, brands, logos, movie/game/anime characters or franchises in any example or prompt. Use original characters and generic archetypes ("a 1970s jazz singer", "a desert courier", "a ceramic coffee mug").
3. Tools change fast. Before naming a tool or a feature, verify it with WebSearch that it exists as of October 2026. Write "as of late 2026" where it matters. Never state exact prices or credit costs unless you verified them, and then add "check current pricing". Prefer principles that survive tool changes.
4. Ethics box where relevant: only use faces of people who consented (yourself or with permission), label AI content where platforms require it (Meta "AI info", TikTok AI label, YouTube altered/synthetic disclosure), never impersonate real people.
5. Free or cheap options first, paid upgrades second.

## Format (Markdown)
- Start with `# Chapter N — Title` then one italic line subtitle.
- `## What you'll make` (3–5 bullets of concrete outputs).
- Body with `##` and `###` sections, numbered steps.
- Copy-ready prompts in fenced blocks with the language tag `prompt`:
  ```prompt
  ...
  ```
  Use [BRACKETS] for the parts the reader swaps. Each prompt must be complete and usable as is.
- Callouts as blockquotes starting with a bold label: `> **Fix it:** ...`, `> **Pro tip:** ...`, `> **Heads up:** ...`, `> **Ethics:** ...`.
- Tables are welcome for comparisons.
- End every chapter with `## Checklist` (5–8 checkbox items `- [ ]`) and `## Try it now` (one 15-minute exercise).
- Length: 3,000–4,000 words per chapter, at least 8 copy-ready prompt blocks per chapter.

## The book (so you can cross-reference)
Part 1 — Foundations
1. Your AI studio: which tool for which job (images, video, editing, upscaling, voice; free vs paid; a one-page "tool cost map")
2. Prompting like a director: prompt anatomy, camera and lens language, light, film looks, structured (JSON) prompts, reference images
3. One face, every shot: consistent characters and identities (reference sheets, using your own selfies, keeping a face stable from photo to video)
Part 2 — Photos
4. Pro portraits from selfies: headshots, lifestyle portraits, restoring and colorizing old photos
5. Product photos without a photoshoot: catalog shots, styled scenes, ad grids from one product photo
Part 3 — Video
6. From still to motion: image-to-video scene by scene, motion prompts, short clips, multi-shot sequences, director logic
7. Six repeatable viral formats + the edit: (party scene, movie-trailer, "me in another era", cinematic product reveal, before/after, POV) and finishing in an editor (cuts, captions, sound, 9:16 export)
Part 4 — Quality
8. Make it not look AI: the 6 tells (plastic skin, hands/teeth/text, too-perfect light, face drift, video morphing, robotic captions/voice) with exact fixes, plus a troubleshooting table of common errors and a final quality checklist
